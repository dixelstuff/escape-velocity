# Choosing Word Golf holes

## Current generation brief

Design the **tee → dogleg → flag triple**, not a sequence of played words. The aim is an approachable hole that allows a satisfying conceptual transition through a short sequence, often two or three player-chosen words. The alternative dogleg landing is often the critical hinge. This is an experience to favour, not a required route or stroke count.

For example, **BOULDER → COMMUNITY → COSTUME** allowed the observed route BOULDER → ROCK → MUSIC → PERFORMER → COSTUME. ROCK shifts naturally from stone to music; **MUSIC** satisfies the COMMUNITY dogleg and provides a useful landing towards COSTUME. The triple offered a discoverable change of context through familiar connections. This illustrates a successful opportunity, not a prescribed route or a requirement for words with multiple meanings.

- **Tee:** favour familiar words that offer several ordinary first thoughts and contexts. Versatile everyday words and ordinary situations led the rated trial; concrete words remain useful. Multiple meanings are a bonus, not a requirement. The first foothold should feel sensible without revealing a routine bridge immediately.
- **Dogleg:** favour a familiar neighbourhood with distinct conceptual possibilities, not merely many loosely related words. The named dogleg need not be played: an alternative landing can connect incoming and onward contexts, with different landings offering different choices. Judge the neighbourhood, not only the literal endpoint pairs. Vary contexts across a batch; avoid repeatedly pairing physical tees with abstract or domain-labelled doglegs.
- **Flag:** favour a recognisable destination with several familiar associations through uses, actions, settings or relationships. Specificity should open possibilities, not create a narrow vocabulary or association bottleneck. Consider likely arrival contexts: ideally the flag rewards reconsidering the approach, without manufacturing difficulty through unrelated words. Be cautious about routine category, colour, size or generic-property finishes; this is a preference, not a ban on word classes.
- **Combination:** prioritise natural exploration over visibly demonstrating a clever design idea. Meaning switches and alternative landings are opportunities to favour, not features every hole must showcase. An obvious opening may offer useful setups or an awkward arrival that invites a recognisable alternative approach; awkwardness alone is not a virtue. Easy arrivals should leave an interesting challenge, not a routine or soft general finish. Broad words are fine with a clear conceptual role. Avoid combinations whose appeal depends on one particular-looking bridge, hidden doorways, forced quirkiness or the same bridge on both legs; cross-leg reuse remains legal. Prescribe neither routes nor extra strokes.
- **Very light checks:** one editorial pass over familiar contexts, then local vocabulary/input-policy, duplicate-triple and direct-leg checks plus six independently nominated familiar opening ideas per tee. Distinguish useful setups from immediate dogleg arrivals. For obvious arrivals, briefly consider the onward challenge editorially. Technically legal but inexplicable moves are insufficient. Do not map landing sets, probe continuations or full routes, simulate play or optimise Cleverness/results. Treat ratings as evidence about early discovery, not the hole's ceiling. Disappointing easy finishes guide selection without proving better routes absent or making every weak route grounds for rejection.
- **Freshness and preservation:** avoid recent word reuse across all three positions, including withdrawn batches the player has seen. Fresh endpoints can still invite stale discoveries: lightly consider familiar route motifs from recent feedback, such as the repeated clothing/legal switch through SUIT, without banning the word or searching routes. Check the recent word list locally; broaden familiar vocabulary rather than reaching for obscurity. New batches appear first in public and admin order, retaining earlier enjoyable holes, permanent IDs and progress unless removal is requested.

## Historical lessons and references

Earlier route-first recipes, exhaustive landing/continuation audits, simulated difficulty targets and welcoming/discovery quotas are superseded by the current brief. Forced quirky vocabulary and overly distant tee/dogleg pairings were unsuccessful directions. Keep the lessons—natural access, meaningful transitions, varied vocabulary and useful landing choices—without reinstating those processes.

Long exploration can be enjoyable, and a quick finish can be satisfying. Legal vector connections and high Cleverness do not establish enjoyment. Preserve examples of good human associations the model misses; do not change mechanics to rescue a hole. Current rules, identity and publishing safeguards live in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).
